01 Driving Safety / Human Performance
Tablet-Based Hazard Anticipation Training for Novice Teen Drivers
Overview

This project evaluated the Engaged Driver Training System (EDTS), a tablet-based hazard anticipation training program for novice teen drivers. The study examined whether training improved drivers' ability to detect, understand, and anticipate latent roadway hazards across simulator and field settings.

Study population/setting
  • Novice teen driver safety study conducted through ATRANS at Western New England University.
  • Combined tablet-based training, driving simulator assessment, and field driving assessment.
Methods and tools
Eye tracking Driving simulation Field driving assessment Commentary driving Verbal protocol analysis Situation awareness Transfer of training
Key contributions or findings
  • Evaluated a tablet-based hazard anticipation training system for novice teen drivers.
  • Assessed roadway scanning, hazard anticipation, and situation awareness.
  • Connected simulator assessment with field driving to evaluate transfer of training.

Tablet-based EDTS training system used to practice identifying latent roadway hazards.

Field driving assessment used to evaluate hazard perception and transfer beyond the simulator.

Driver point-of-view hazard scene used to assess roadway scanning, hazard anticipation, and situation awareness.

02 Aviation Training / Human Performance
Design and Evaluation of Gaze-Based Training for General Aviation Pilots
Overview

This project designed and evaluated a gaze-based training intervention for general aviation pilots during inadvertent transitions from visual flight rules into instrument meteorological conditions. The work used eye tracking, flight simulation, expert gaze, and commentary to assess changes in visual scanning, situation awareness, transfer of training, and aircraft-control outcomes.

Study population/setting
  • General aviation student pilot training research conducted in a flight simulator setting.
  • Focused on VFR-into-IMC transition scenarios and instrument scanning behavior.
Methods and tools
Eye tracking Flight simulation Expert gaze Expert commentary 3M training Shannon entropy Kullback-Leibler divergence
Key contributions or findings
  • Designed and evaluated a gaze-based aviation training intervention.
  • Assessed visual scanning, situation awareness, and transfer of training.
  • After training, experimental pilots increased attention to primary instruments.
  • After training, experimental pilots reduced visual search randomness and maintained aircraft control during IMC scenarios.

Eye-tracking setup used for gaze-based pilot training and data collection.

Before Training: Pre-training gaze distribution showing dispersed visual attention across cockpit instruments.

After Training: Post-training gaze distribution showing more focused visual attention on primary flight instruments.

03 Aviation Analytics / Vocal Stress
Multimodal Assessment of Pilot Performance and Vocal Stress
Overview

This project developed a multimodal framework to evaluate whether aviation training effects extended beyond visual scanning behavior. The analysis synchronized gaze, flight-control, aircraft-state, performance, and voice data to assess pilot performance and vocal stress during simulated VFR-into-IMC flight.

Methods and tools
Data synchronization Eye tracking Flight-control data Aircraft-state data Voice data Multimodal machine learning SHAP CNN vocal-stress analysis
Key contributions or findings
  • Synchronized eye-movement, flight-control, aircraft-state, performance, and voice data.
  • Used multimodal machine-learning classification to assess training-related differences.
  • Used SHAP-based interpretation to examine model behavior.
  • Analyzed voice data separately using a CNN-based vocal stress model.
  • Assessed training effects beyond visual scanning behavior.

Pilot data synchronization workflow linking eye-movement, voice, flight-control, aircraft-state, and flight performance data.

Multimodal framework for assessing pilot training effectiveness and vocal stress using synchronized flight, gaze, and voice data.

04 Healthcare Human Factors / Clinical Workflow
Naturalistic Stress and Workload Monitoring in ICU Nurses
Overview

This research examined stress, workload, and physiological responses among intensive care unit nurses during real clinical shifts. The broader program connected naturalistic workload monitoring with longitudinal work on occupational stress and burnout among ICU nurses during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Study population/setting
  • Physiological data from 23 cardiovascular ICU nurses were analyzed in one study.
  • Eye-tracking data from 21 ICU nurses, and complete eye-tracking plus physiological data from 15 nurses, were collected across day and night shifts in a related workload study.
  • Longitudinal work included COVID and non-COVID units, repeated 12-hour shifts, validated questionnaires, wearable physiological data, and qualitative shift-level responses.
Methods and tools
Naturalistic clinical study design Empatica E4 Tobii Pro Glasses 2 Axivity AX3 Baevsky Stress Index Gaze entropy Mixed-effects modeling Ordinal regression
Key contributions or findings
  • Analyzed physiological stress signals including heart rate, electrodermal activity, and skin temperature.
  • Examined workload and stress through gaze behavior, fixation patterns, pupil diameter, gaze entropy, and Baevsky Stress Index.
  • Connected wearable sensing, eye tracking, validated questionnaires, and qualitative shift-level responses in clinical research.

Empatica E4 wrist sensor used to collect physiological stress signals including heart rate, electrodermal activity, and skin temperature.

Postdoctoral research workspace at Houston Methodist Center for Outcomes Research.

05 Digital Health / Healthcare Worker Well-Being
Digital Positive-Emotion Intervention for Healthcare Worker Mental Health
Overview

This project evaluated a mobile positive-emotion intervention delivered through the Virgin Pulse platform for healthcare workers. The analysis examined changes in anxiety, depression, positive emotion, negative emotion, emotional diversity, and burnout.

Study population/setting
  • The primary sample included 137 healthcare workers.
  • Fifty participants had measurements at pre-, mid-, and post-intervention.
Methods and tools
Virgin Pulse platform Mobile intervention Positive emotion Negative emotion Emotional diversity Burnout Longitudinal mixed-effects modeling
Key contributions or findings
  • Anxiety and depression decreased significantly.
  • Positive emotion increased and negative emotion decreased significantly.
  • Negative emotional diversity decreased.
  • Burnout outcomes were not statistically significant.
06 Healthcare Human Factors / Clinical Workflow
Hierarchical Task Analysis of Ultrasound-Guided Vascular Access Procedures
Overview

This project examined ultrasound-guided vascular access procedures performed in a simulation center using first-person video, procedural timing, and hierarchical task analysis. The analysis supports future work on procedural efficiency, Lean process improvement, targeted microskill training, and ergonomic assessment using RULA.

Study population/setting
  • Five fellowship-trained emergency medicine physicians performed four procedures on high-fidelity simulators.
  • Procedures included peripheral IV placement, single-lumen midline catheter placement, dual-lumen midline catheter placement, and triple-lumen central venous catheter placement.
Methods and tools
Hierarchical task analysis Simulation-based assessment Tobii Pro Glasses 2 First-person video analysis Procedural timing Microskill identification RULA assessment
Key contributions or findings
  • Identified supraordinate tasks, subtasks, and microskills required for each procedure.
  • Connected workflow analysis with procedural timing and simulation-based assessment.
  • Supported future work on procedural efficiency, Lean process improvement, microskill training, and ergonomics.

08 Digital Health UX/UI / Patient Engagement
User-Centered Design of a Digital Patient Navigation Tool for Breast Cancer Care
Overview

This Stand Up To Cancer project focused on the user-centered design and UX/UI evaluation of a culturally tailored, app-enhanced patient navigation tool for breast cancer care. The work examined patient needs, hormonal therapy adherence, side-effect monitoring, patient-provider communication workflows, and access to support resources.

Study population/setting
  • Patient-facing digital health work for Black/African American women with ER+ breast cancer.
  • Conducted during a postdoctoral fellowship at Houston Methodist Center for Outcomes Research.
Methods and tools
User-centered design UX/UI evaluation Stakeholder interviews Thematic analysis MAXQDA Functional requirements Prototype development Usability testing
Key contributions or findings
  • Evaluated patient-facing digital health design and engagement considerations.
  • Supported prototype development and formative usability testing of CareSense pathway and mobile application concepts.
  • Connected patient needs assessment, communication workflow analysis, and health equity considerations.
